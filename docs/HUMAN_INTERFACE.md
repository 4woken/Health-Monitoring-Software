# Human interface: direction and limits

The body appears with opaque skin, natural light and a steady pose, in front of a picture of the room where the person lives. Colour appears only where attention is needed. The depth control offers EVA (spacewalk suit), IVA (flight suit), Skin, Vessels, Organs and Skeleton. When you select a body part, it stays in view, with a pop-up beside it and the details in a panel on the right.

## One screen

Everything happens around the body. Panels open over it and close back to it; the body slides aside to stay in view.

1. Choosing an existing member in the opening crew list or the dashboard menu opens that member’s dashboard directly. Avatar setup is available when adding a member or from the crew menu. It shows a close-up 3D head and offers local webcam fitting. **Next** saves the fitted face to that profile in this browser. The dashboard loads it on later visits with the camera off.
2. **Top bar.** The Pulsar logo, then the person's initials and short name, small, with a menu. The menu switches person, shows the call sign, speciality and flight history of the person shown, and offers **Put my face on the body**, **Edit body dimensions** and **Add a crew member**. On the right: **Simulation**, **AR view**, **Check-up** with the number of tasks left, **Tests**, and the destination button with the mission day, which opens the mission settings (the mission day, the four destinations and the five habitats). While the camera runs, a button with the live pulse stops it. While the simulation runs, its playback sits in the middle of the bar.
3. **The body.** The controls run down the left side: the **Depth** control with its six stops, from the EVA and IVA suits through Skin, Vessels and Organs to Skeleton, **Mission effects**, which shows the result of the physiology engine on the body, then **Head**, **Chest**, **Whole body**, the zoom buttons and **NASA reference**. The notifications run down the right side: the destination and day, "N things to do today" as cards in the colour of their urgency, red to act now and amber for today, and **See all N signals**; on phones the list folds. Markers on the body are white, amber or red. **+** zooms toward the chest, then the head, and every zoom keeps the body on its axis: the wheel keeps the height under the pointer, never moves the body sideways. A marker that would fall behind the vital signs hides, so its name never covers them. Along the bottom, the vital signs (heart rate, breathing, blood oxygen, blood pressure, temperature, blood sugar, sleep and radiation) each open their details, and the heart-rate card can start the camera with **Measure the pulse with the camera**. A value outside the limits of its signal turns amber or red, with a dot and a word (Low, High, Fever, Short); the breathing estimate stays white, since it is experimental. The breathing card opens the breathing-rate test. The credits of the models and photographs are in the README and `docs/CREDITS.md`, not over the scene.
4. **Pop-up.** A click on a recommendation, a marker or a place on the body moves the camera there and opens a card beside that part: the state, the value and its trend over 30 days, what to do and why, and the NASA exam on the same question with its in-flight schedule. It offers **Details**, **Add to the check-up** (or **In the check-up**, which opens the check-up, once it is there) and the self-test on the same question, such as **Take the reaction test** for sleep and alertness or **Test my vision** for the optic nerve. An organ shown on its own offers its own test too. The card never covers the notifications on the right: the body slides left to make room, but never under the controls on the left. Escape or the cross closes it.
5. **Panels on the right.**
   - The **details** of a signal: "Today", "What it is", "What it means for you", "How it is measured" and "What to do". Then where the value comes from and how old it is, **Add to today's check-up** when the signal needs attention (or **In today's check-up**, which opens it), and a chart since launch. Then what NASA examines on the same question, and "Sources" with each published source and the NASA risk page. On the Moon and Mars, the signals whose limits come from weightless flights carry a note about partial gravity.
   - An **organ**, clicked on its own: "What it does", "In space" and the signals followed there.
   - **All signals**, with **Trends**, a chart of up to three measurements since launch.
   - The **NASA reference** of the destination: gravity, radiation, light and UV, the closed cabin, and distance and isolation. Each says what it does here and what NASA does about it, then come all 47 requirements ([NASA_REFERENCE.md](NASA_REFERENCE.md)).
   - **Mission effects**: the physiology simulator, while the body shows its four callouts.
6. **Check-up.** It slides in from the right: "Today's check-up" with a progress bar, then "Exams and NASA schedule", "Self-tests", "Follow-ups from the signals", "Every day" and "Added by hand". Each task can be ticked, removed, or shown with **See it on the body**, which closes the panel and opens the pop-up of that part. Once every task of a signal is ticked, its marker on the body turns back to normal and it leaves the list of things to do. The check-up closes when a panel or a pop-up of the body opens, and the other way round, so they never overlap. A self-test task has **Start**, and ticks itself once the test is done. The habitat exercise demo comes last: **Start avatar demo** plays the movement on the body beside the panel. **Add a task for today** adds one by hand, and **Plan again** rebuilds the planned tasks.
7. **Tests.** It covers the scene, and the body stops drawing underneath. "For today" lists the self-tests that are due, with why: NASA's schedule, a signal, the first week after a change of gravity, or a run to repeat within 24 hours. Then come the tests and the exercises, each with its last result and the person's usual one. A test opens in three steps: **Prepare** (why today, what it measures, how it goes, what you need, a safety note for anything physical, what NASA does and how this version differs, a 3D figure for movements, and a choice such as the camera or the phone for balance), the test itself (the mirrored camera with the skeleton, large words to read from a few metres away, a voice from this device, a timer or a counter), and the **Result** (the score, the comparison with the usual result and with the last run, where the result goes on the body, and **Do it again**). The Lab at the bottom keeps the camera tools.
8. **Face fitting studio** (in the Lab). **Face & body** shows the face live on the 3D head and gives guidance on framing, head angle, light and stability before **Capture & review**; these checks are visual rules of thumb. The fit can then be adjusted (**Face size**, **Left / right**, **Height**, **3D face fit**, **Skin colour blend**, **Auto-fit face**), and **Use this face** lays the still portrait on the body, with the camera off. **Try a sample person** and **Play sample motion** run the same steps on a fictional portrait that stays labelled as such. **Pulse camera** estimates the pulse from the skin of the face ([VALIDATION.md](VALIDATION.md)). **Back to the body** closes the tools.
9. **AR view.** A simulated helmet visor on a spacewalk, over the whole screen: a photographic helmet frame with the two microphones in front, and a generated view outside for each destination (a station above the Earth, a transit ship with Mars in the distance, the lunar south pole, Gale crater). The glass carries the PULSAR mark and the suit's links and battery at the top, the menu under them (**Hide info** and the four destinations), and the readings along its lower rim: heart rate with its trace, blood oxygen, blood pressure, the next drink, temperature, blood sugar and sleep. A value out of range takes its warning colour, label and number, with the limits of the dashboard; the heart rate uses spacewalk limits (amber from 150, red from 170) and the dose rate turns red at five times the usual rate. The readings and the menu keep their place on the glass on any screen. **Back** is in the lower left corner, **Eye control on** in the lower right, with **Recalibrate** beside it once the calibration is done. No other text covers the view. Eye control starts with the view: nine dots appear one after the other, each with a ring that closes on it, then a bright ring follows the gaze. On a button it shrinks, the button lights up and a bar fills along its foot; after a second the button is pressed. With eye control on, the buttons are larger and further apart. **Back** answers only to the mouse and the keyboard. [How it works](EYE_CONTROL.md).
10. **Simulation.** It plays a day of the destination on the dashboard itself. The playback sits in the top bar: **Play** or **Pause**, the clock and the current activity, the bar of the day (one block per activity, as tall as its effort, filled up to now, with a dot for each warning; it can be clicked or dragged), the speed (5, 15 or 2 minutes a second), **Next event** and the cross that ends it. The vital signs along the bottom say **Live** and show the minute's values, and the 3D heart beats at that rate. The markers and the list of things to do follow the minute: the dosimeter turns red in the solar storm, the air amber when the scrubber stops. At the top of the notifications, the clock, the current activity and the latest news. **Check-up** shows the list of the simulated day, ticked as it goes, with the tasks the alerts add (highlighted for a while, with the time they were added), the moved ones (with why) and **Take the test** for a self-test; under it, everything that happened. **AR view** shows the same minute in the helmet ([how the simulation works](SIMULATION.md)).
11. **Add a crew member** offers **Create profile** to open the dashboard immediately and **Continue to face setup** to fit a portrait first. **Edit body dimensions** in the crew menu revises height and weight at any time.

## Portrait data

**Next** in avatar setup and **Use this face** in the studio save the cropped images and their fit to the selected profile on this device. Pulsar keeps no webcam recording and does not sync portraits across browsers or devices. To erase the saved face, choose **Remove portrait** in the studio or select **Use reference head** and confirm with **Next** in avatar setup. Deleting an added member erases their portrait. Clearing the site's data erases every saved portrait.

The portrait is projected on the geometry of the head and its eyes, so the head and the neck stay continuous under the same lights. The eye landmarks align it, its edges are softened, and a colour sampled on the cheeks tints the skin of the body, as far as **Skin colour blend** allows. The personal shape of the skull, the hair, the organs and the silhouette are not rebuilt. The portrait is not used to recognise anyone.

## Rendering and resources

- The spacecraft and space station habitats are **NASA photographs**: an Orion mock-up on Earth, and the Destiny laboratory in orbit. The rover, Moon base and Mars base interiors are **illustrations generated with NASA photographs as visual references**: SEV, Orion, HDU and CHAPEA for the structures, Apollo 17 and Curiosity for the landscapes.
- The habitat menu says which pictures are photographs and which are generated illustrations. The original sources, the full credits, which photographs fed which illustration and the exact prompts are in the README and in `GENERATION.md`, both in `public/environments/`. The illustrations are not photographs of real bases or validated NASA designs, and the source images are unchanged.
- The anatomy models keep their attributions, listed in the README and `docs/CREDITS.md`. Triangles whose orientation contradicts their normals are corrected on loading, to avoid lighting artefacts.
- The two bodies are textured MakeHuman bodies (CC0) with the anatomy registered inside them. If they cannot load, the BodyParts3D reference bodies take their place. Skin is the default depth. From there you can move outward to the IVA and EVA suits or inward to the vessels, organs and skeleton. The active system stays opaque and the surrounding anatomy remains faint. The selector lists the meshes supplied by the active atlas, including organs occluded in the current view. The female pelvis and major leg bones come from the female HRA. The reference vessels include the named lower-limb veins from Z-Anatomy. [Model credits](../THIRD_PARTY_NOTICES.md).
- The heart always beats and the chest always breathes. The heart follows the person's resting pulse, the camera pulse while the camera measures it, or the minute of the mission simulation. The chest follows the latest breathing rate measured with the camera (**Tests → Breathing rate**), the simulated one, or 14 breaths a minute otherwise. Only the rate can be measured: the depth and phase of the movement are illustrative.
- With **Mission effects** on, a slight change in cheek fullness and calf contour illustrates the projected fluid shift.

## Still missing

A full photorealistic double of the body would need suitable textured and rigged models, and a further capture step. The current fit improves how a portrait sits on the generic head, but the hair, the back of the head and the personal shape are still missing. The avatar can demonstrate five environment-dependent movements. Its exercise response is illustrative. A dedicated muscle layer and personalised muscle geometry remain outside this model. The heart follows the camera pulse and the chest the breathing rate measured by the camera; the depth and phase of these movements are illustrative. The plan for realistic bodies is in [ROADMAP.md](ROADMAP.md).

The detection and the fit were checked in the browser with the fictional sample. A capture with a physical webcam still has to be checked on the user's own hardware. The automatic tests check the alignment, the outlines, the capture limits and the projection, not how good the result looks on each face.
