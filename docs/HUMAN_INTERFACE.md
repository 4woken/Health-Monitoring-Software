# Human interface: direction and limits

The body appears with opaque skin, natural light and a steady pose, in front of a picture of the room where the person lives. Colour appears only where attention is needed. The depth control offers EVA (spacewalk suit), IVA (flight suit), Skin, Vessels, Organs and Skeleton. When you select a body part, it stays in view, with a pop-up beside it and the details in a panel on the right.

## One screen

Everything happens around the body. Panels open over it and close back to it; the body slides aside to stay in view.

1. Choosing an existing member in the opening crew list or the dashboard menu opens that member’s dashboard directly. Avatar setup is available when adding a member or from the crew menu. It shows a close-up 3D head and offers local webcam fitting. **Next** saves the fitted face to that profile in this browser. The dashboard loads it on later visits with the camera off.
2. **Top bar.** The Pulsar logo, then the person's initials and short name, small, with a menu. The menu switches person, shows the call sign, speciality and flight history of the person shown, and offers **Put my face on the body**, **Edit body dimensions** and **Add a crew member**. On the right: **AR view**, **Check-up** with the number of tasks left, **Tests**, and the destination button with the mission day, which opens the mission settings (the mission day, the four destinations and the five habitats). While the camera runs, a button with the live pulse stops it.
3. **The body.** At the top left: the destination and day, "N things to do today" and their list, **See all N signals** and **NASA reference**. The three most urgent recommendations also point at the body as cards, red to act now and amber for today; on phones the list folds and the cards stay off. Markers on the body are white, amber or red. The **Depth** control has six stops from EVA through IVA, Skin, Vessels and Organs to Skeleton, and **Mission effects** shows the result of the physiology engine on the body. **Body motion** (Still, Heartbeat, Breathing), **Head**, **Chest**, **Whole body** and the zoom buttons change the view. Along the bottom, the vital signs (heart rate, blood oxygen, blood pressure, temperature, blood sugar, sleep) each open their details, and the heart-rate card can start the camera with **Measure the pulse with the camera**. The breathing card opens the breathing-rate test. The credits of the models and photographs are in the README and `docs/CREDITS.md`, not over the scene.
4. **Pop-up.** A click on a recommendation, a marker or a place on the body moves the camera there and opens a card beside that part: the state, the value and its trend over 30 days, what to do and why, and the NASA exam on the same question with its in-flight schedule. It offers **Details**, **Add to the check-up** and the self-test on the same question, such as **Take the reaction test** for sleep and alertness or **Test my vision** for the optic nerve. An organ shown on its own offers its own test too. Escape or the cross closes it.
5. **Panels on the right.**
   - The **details** of a signal: "Value", "What it is", "What it means here", "How it is taken" and "What to do". Then where the value comes from and how old it is, **Add to today's check-up** when the signal needs attention, and a chart since launch. Then what NASA examines on the same question, and "Why we say so" with each source and the NASA risk page. On the Moon and Mars, the signals whose limits come from weightless flights carry a note about partial gravity.
   - An **organ**, clicked on its own: "What it does", "In space" and the signals followed there.
   - **All signals**, with **Trends**, a chart of up to three measurements since launch.
   - The **NASA reference** of the destination: gravity, radiation, light and UV, the closed cabin, and distance and isolation. Each says what it does here and what NASA does about it, then come all 47 requirements ([NASA_REFERENCE.md](NASA_REFERENCE.md)).
   - **Mission effects**: the physiology simulator, while the body shows its four callouts.
6. **Check-up.** It slides in from the right: "Today's check-up" with a progress bar, then "Exams and NASA schedule", "Self-tests", "Follow-ups from the signals", "Every day" and "Added by hand". Each task can be ticked, removed, or shown with **See it on the body**, which closes the panel and opens the pop-up of that part. A self-test task has **Start**, and ticks itself once the test is done. The habitat exercise demo comes last: **Start avatar demo** plays the movement on the body beside the panel. **Add a task for today** adds one by hand, and **Plan again** rebuilds the planned tasks.
7. **Tests.** It covers the scene, and the body stops drawing underneath. "For today" lists the self-tests that are due, with why: NASA's schedule, a signal, the first week after a change of gravity, or a run to repeat within 24 hours. Then come the tests and the exercises, each with its last result and the person's usual one. A test opens in three steps: **Prepare** (why today, what it measures, how it goes, what you need, a safety note for anything physical, what NASA does and how this version differs, a 3D figure for movements, and a choice such as the camera or the phone for balance), the test itself (the mirrored camera with the skeleton, large words to read from a few metres away, a voice from this device, a timer or a counter), and the **Result** (the score, the comparison with the usual result and with the last run, where the result goes on the body, and **Do it again**). The Lab at the bottom keeps the camera tools.
8. **Face fitting studio** (in the Lab). **Face & body** shows the face live on the 3D head and gives guidance on framing, head angle, light and stability before **Capture & review**; these checks are visual rules of thumb. The fit can then be adjusted (**Face size**, **Left / right**, **Height**, **3D face fit**, **Skin colour blend**, **Auto-fit face**), and **Use this face** lays the still portrait on the body, with the camera off. **Try a sample person** and **Play sample motion** run the same steps on a fictional portrait that stays labelled as such. **Pulse camera** estimates the pulse from the skin of the face ([VALIDATION.md](VALIDATION.md)). **Back to the body** closes the tools.
9. **AR view.** A simulated helmet visor on a spacewalk, over the whole screen: the helmet brim above and the lower rim below, out of focus, and NASA photographs outside (the Earth from the station, Orion with the Earth and the Moon in the distance, Apollo 17 on the Moon, Curiosity on Mars). Only health shows: the pulse and its trace, breathing, oxygen, core temperature, effort and suit CO2 for the spacewalk, and what to check today. **Back**, **Hide info** (the view clears and sharpens), the four missions, and **Eye control on**. Eye control starts with the view: nine dots appear one after the other, each with a ring that closes on it, then a bright ring follows the gaze. On a button it shrinks, the button lights up and a bar fills along its foot; after a second the button is pressed. With eye control on, the buttons are larger and further apart. **Back** answers only to the mouse and the keyboard. The line under the buttons gives the typical error and offers **Recalibrate** ([how it works](EYE_CONTROL.md)).
10. **Add a crew member** offers **Create profile** to open the dashboard immediately and **Continue to face setup** to fit a portrait first. **Avatar dimensions** on the dashboard lets the person revise height and weight at any time.

## Portrait data

**Next** in avatar setup and **Use this face** in the studio save the cropped images and their fit to the selected profile on this device. Pulsar keeps no webcam recording and does not sync portraits across browsers or devices. To erase the saved face, choose **Remove portrait** in the studio or select **Use reference head** and confirm with **Next** in avatar setup. Deleting an added member erases their portrait. Clearing the site's data erases every saved portrait.

The portrait is projected on the geometry of the head and its eyes, so the head and the neck stay continuous under the same lights. The eye landmarks align it, its edges are softened, and a colour sampled on the cheeks tints the skin of the body, as far as **Skin colour blend** allows. The personal shape of the skull, the hair, the organs and the silhouette are not rebuilt. The portrait is not used to recognise anyone.

## Rendering and resources

- The spacecraft and space station habitats are **NASA photographs**: an Orion mock-up on Earth, and the Destiny laboratory in orbit. The rover, Moon base and Mars base interiors are **illustrations generated with NASA photographs as visual references**: SEV, Orion, HDU and CHAPEA for the structures, Apollo 17 and Curiosity for the landscapes.
- The habitat menu says which pictures are photographs and which are generated illustrations. The original sources, the full credits, which photographs fed which illustration and the exact prompts are in the README and in `GENERATION.md`, both in `public/environments/`. The illustrations are not photographs of real bases or validated NASA designs, and the source images are unchanged.
- The anatomy models keep their attributions, shown under the body. Triangles whose orientation contradicts their normals are corrected on loading, to avoid lighting artefacts.
- The two bodies are textured MakeHuman bodies (CC0) with the anatomy registered inside them. If they cannot load, the BodyParts3D reference bodies take their place. Skin is the default depth. From there you can move outward to the IVA and EVA suits or inward to the vessels, organs and skeleton. The active system stays opaque and the surrounding anatomy remains faint. The selector lists the meshes supplied by the active atlas, including organs occluded in the current view. The female pelvis and major leg bones come from the female HRA. The reference vessels include the named lower-limb veins from Z-Anatomy. [Model credits](../THIRD_PARTY_NOTICES.md).
- The heart beats at the person's simulated resting pulse, or at the camera pulse while the camera measures it. **Breathing** moves the chest at the person's latest breathing rate measured with the camera (**Tests → Breathing rate**); without a measurement the chest stays still. Only the rate is measured: the depth and phase of the movement are illustrative.
- With **Mission effects** on, the change of the face and the legs from the fluid shift is exaggerated about tenfold, so that it can be seen.

## Still missing

A full photorealistic double of the body would need suitable textured and rigged models, and a further capture step. The current fit improves how a portrait sits on the generic head, but the hair, the back of the head and the personal shape are still missing. The avatar can demonstrate five environment-dependent movements. Its exercise response is illustrative. A dedicated muscle layer and personalised muscle geometry remain outside this model. The heart follows the camera pulse and the chest the breathing rate measured by the camera; the depth and phase of these movements are illustrative. The plan for realistic bodies is in [ROADMAP.md](ROADMAP.md).

The detection and the fit were checked in the browser with the fictional sample. A capture with a physical webcam still has to be checked on the user's own hardware. The automatic tests check the alignment, the outlines, the capture limits and the projection, not how good the result looks on each face.
