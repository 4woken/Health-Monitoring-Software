# Human interface: direction and limits

The body appears with opaque skin, natural light and a steady pose, in front of a picture of the room where the person lives. Colour appears only where attention is needed. The layers under the skin are chosen with the depth control; a selected place stays in view, and its explanation opens in a panel on the right.

## Screens

1. **Crew list.** The first screen asks "Who's on board?" and lists Lucas, Enzo, Nassim and Youssef, then anyone added in this browser, each with their role and either "All clear" or the number of signals to watch. Nobody signs in, and every profile is open to everyone. A role (Commander, Pilot, Flight engineer, Mission specialist, Scientist or Medic) is a job on board and never changes what someone can see.
2. **Adding someone.** **Add a crew member** asks for a name, a role on board and a reference body (Male or Female). **Next: the face** opens the face studio. **Add without a face**, or **Skip the face for now** in the studio, keeps the reference face. The camera stays off until the person starts it.
3. **Top bar.** The Pulsar logo goes back to the crew list. **Body**, **Check-up** and **Camera** switch screens; on phones and tablets they sit in a tab bar at the bottom. Check-up shows how many tasks are left. The destination button shows the destination and the mission day, and opens the mission settings: the mission day, the four destinations and the five habitats. The round button with the person's face or initials opens the crew menu. While the camera runs, a button with the live pulse stops it.
4. **Body.** At the top left: the crew, to switch person, then the person's name and role, the destination and day, how many signals need attention, **All N signals**, **Put my face on it** (or **Change the face**), and up to four signals that need attention. Markers on the body are white, amber or red. The **Depth** control goes from Skin to Vessels, Organs and Skeleton, and **Mission effects** shows the result of the physiology engine on the body. **Body motion** (Still, Heartbeat, Breathing), **Head**, **Chest**, **Whole body** and the zoom buttons change the view. Along the bottom, the vital signs (heart rate, blood oxygen, blood pressure, temperature, blood sugar, sleep) each open their signal, and the heart-rate card can start the camera with **Measure the pulse with the camera**. Under the scene: the physiology simulator, "Where each signal is measured", and a chart of up to three measurements since launch.
5. **Signal panel.** A click on a marker, a vital sign or a place on the body moves the camera there and opens the panel: "Value", "What it is", "What it means here", "How it is taken" and "What to do", then where the value comes from and how old it is, **Add to today's check-up** when the signal needs attention, a chart since launch, and "Why we say so" with each source and the NASA risk page. On the Moon and Mars, the signals whose limits come from weightless flights carry a note about partial gravity. A click on an organ shows it alone, with "What it does", "In space" and the signals followed there.
6. **Check-up.** "Name's check-up" with a progress bar, then "Exams", "Follow-ups from the signals", "Every day" and "Added by hand". Each task can be ticked, removed, or traced back to the body with **See it on the body**. **Add a task for today** adds one by hand, and **Plan again** rebuilds the planned tasks.
7. **Camera.** **Face on the body** shows the face live on the 3D head and gives guidance on framing, head angle, light and stability before **Capture & review**; these checks are visual rules of thumb. The fit can then be adjusted (**Face size**, **Left / right**, **Height**, **3D face fit**, **Skin colour blend**, **Auto-fit face**), and **Use this face** lays the still portrait on the body, with the camera off. **Try a sample person** and **Play sample motion** run the same steps on a fictional portrait that stays labelled as such. **Pulse** estimates the pulse from the skin of the face ([VALIDATION.md](VALIDATION.md)).

## Portrait data

The image and its points stay in the React state of the tab once the person chooses **Use this face**. They are never uploaded and never written to the browser's storage, and a reload clears them. A portrait belongs to the person it was captured for; removing that person from the crew removes it too.

The portrait is projected on the geometry of the head and its eyes, so the head and the neck stay continuous under the same lights. The eye landmarks align it, its edges are softened, and a colour sampled on the cheeks tints the skin of the body, as far as **Skin colour blend** allows. The personal shape of the skull, the hair, the organs and the silhouette are not rebuilt. The portrait is not used to recognise anyone.

## Rendering and resources

- The spacecraft and space station habitats are **NASA photographs**: an Orion mock-up on Earth, and the Destiny laboratory in orbit. The rover, Moon base and Mars base interiors are **illustrations generated with NASA photographs as visual references**: SEV, Orion, HDU and CHAPEA for the structures, Apollo 17 and Curiosity for the landscapes.
- The caption under the body says whether the picture is a photograph or a generated illustration, and links to a NASA page. The original sources, the full credits, which photographs fed which illustration and the exact prompts are in the README and in `GENERATION.md`, both in `public/environments/`. The illustrations are not photographs of real bases or validated NASA designs, and the source images are unchanged.
- The anatomy models keep their attributions, shown under the body. Triangles whose orientation contradicts their normals are corrected on loading, to avoid lighting artefacts.
- Skin is the default depth. Vessels, organs and the skeleton can be explored. The male reference body has a full skeleton and all the arteries and veins of BodyParts3D. BodyParts3D has only a short segment of the internal jugular vein, so both internal jugular veins are drawn along the carotid arteries. The female reference body gets the same bones and vessels, bent into her shape by `scripts/fit-female-body.mjs`: an approximation, in which her pelvis keeps the male shape, only widened.
- The heart beats at the person's simulated resting pulse, or at the camera pulse while the camera measures it. **Breathing** moves the chest at the person's latest breathing rate measured with the camera (Camera, **Breathing**); without a measurement the chest stays still. Only the rate is measured: the depth and phase of the movement are illustrative.
- With **Mission effects** on, the change of the face and the legs from the fluid shift is exaggerated about tenfold, so that it can be seen.

## Still missing

A full photorealistic double of the body would need suitable textured and rigged models, and a further capture step. The current fit improves how a portrait sits on the generic head, but the hair, the back of the head and the personal shape are still missing. Muscles and exercise tutorials with an animated body are not available yet. The heart follows the camera pulse and the chest the breathing rate measured by the camera; the depth and phase of these movements are illustrative. The plan for realistic bodies is in [ROADMAP.md](ROADMAP.md).

The detection and the fit were checked in the browser with the fictional sample. A capture with a physical webcam still has to be checked on the user's own hardware. The automatic tests check the alignment, the outlines, the capture limits and the projection, not how good the result looks on each face.
