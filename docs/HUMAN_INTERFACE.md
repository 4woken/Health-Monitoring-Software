# Human interface: direction and limits

The body appears with opaque skin, natural light and a steady pose, in front of a picture of the room where the person lives. Colour appears only where attention is needed. The layers under the skin are chosen with the depth control; a selected place stays in view, with a pop-up beside it and the details in a panel on the right.

## One screen

Everything happens around the body. Panels open over it and close back to it; the body slides aside to stay in view.

1. The opening crew list leads to a close-up 3D head. The crew member can choose their reference body, height and weight, enable local webcam fitting and then select **Next** to enter the dashboard. The fitted image stays on that profile for this session. All profiles are accessible on this device.
2. **Top bar.** The Pulsar logo, then the person's initials and short name, small, with a menu. The menu switches person, shows the call sign, speciality and flight history of the person shown, and offers **Put my face on the body**, **Edit body dimensions** and **Add a crew member**. On the right: **Check-up** with the number of tasks left, **Camera**, and the destination button with the mission day, which opens the mission settings (the mission day, the four destinations and the five habitats). While the camera runs, a button with the live pulse stops it.
3. **The body.** At the top left: the destination and day, "N things to do today" and their list, **See all N signals** and **NASA reference**. The three most urgent recommendations also point at the body as cards, red to act now and amber for today; on phones the list folds and the cards stay off. Markers on the body are white, amber or red. The **Depth** control goes from Skin to Vessels, Organs and Skeleton, and **Mission effects** shows the result of the physiology engine on the body. **Body motion** (Still, Heartbeat, Breathing), **Head**, **Chest**, **Whole body** and the zoom buttons change the view. Along the bottom, the vital signs (heart rate, blood oxygen, blood pressure, temperature, blood sugar, sleep) each open their details, and the heart-rate card can start the camera with **Measure the pulse with the camera**.
4. **Pop-up.** A click on a recommendation, a marker or a place on the body moves the camera there and opens a card beside that part: the state, the value and its trend over 30 days, what to do and why, and the NASA exam on the same question with its in-flight schedule. It offers **Details**, **Add to the check-up** and, for sleep and mood, the matching exercise. Escape or the cross closes it.
5. **Panels on the right.**
   - The **details** of a signal: "Value", "What it is", "What it means here", "How it is taken" and "What to do". Then where the value comes from and how old it is, **Add to today's check-up** when the signal needs attention, and a chart since launch. Then what NASA examines on the same question, and "Why we say so" with each source and the NASA risk page. On the Moon and Mars, the signals whose limits come from weightless flights carry a note about partial gravity.
   - An **organ**, clicked on its own: "What it does", "In space" and the signals followed there.
   - **All signals**, with **Trends**, a chart of up to three measurements since launch.
   - The **NASA reference** of the destination: gravity, radiation, light and UV, the closed cabin, and distance and isolation. Each says what it does here and what NASA does about it, then come all 47 requirements ([NASA_REFERENCE.md](NASA_REFERENCE.md)).
   - **Mission effects**: the physiology simulator, while the body shows its four callouts.
6. **Check-up.** It slides in from the right: "Today's check-up" with a progress bar, the personal mini-missions, then "Exams and NASA schedule", "Follow-ups from the signals", "Every day" and "Added by hand". Each task can be ticked, removed, or shown with **See it on the body**, which closes the panel and opens the pop-up of that part. **Add a task for today** adds one by hand, and **Plan again** rebuilds the planned tasks.
7. **Camera.** It covers the scene, and the body stops drawing underneath. **Face & body** shows the face live on the 3D head and gives guidance on framing, head angle, light and stability before **Capture & review**; these checks are visual rules of thumb. The fit can then be adjusted (**Face size**, **Left / right**, **Height**, **3D face fit**, **Skin colour blend**, **Auto-fit face**), and **Use this face** lays the still portrait on the body, with the camera off. **Try a sample person** and **Play sample motion** run the same steps on a fictional portrait that stays labelled as such. **Pulse camera** estimates the pulse from the skin of the face ([VALIDATION.md](VALIDATION.md)), and **Breathing** the breathing rate from the chest. **Back to the body** closes the tools.
8. **Add a crew member** creates a profile and opens the same 3D setup. **Avatar dimensions** on the dashboard lets the person revise height and weight at any time.

## Portrait data

The image and its points stay in the React state of the tab once the person chooses **Use this face**. They are never uploaded and never written to the browser's storage, and a reload clears them. A portrait belongs to the person it was captured for; removing that person from the crew removes it too.

The portrait is projected on the geometry of the head and its eyes, so the head and the neck stay continuous under the same lights. The eye landmarks align it, its edges are softened, and a colour sampled on the cheeks tints the skin of the body, as far as **Skin colour blend** allows. The personal shape of the skull, the hair, the organs and the silhouette are not rebuilt. The portrait is not used to recognise anyone.

## Rendering and resources

- The spacecraft and space station habitats are **NASA photographs**: an Orion mock-up on Earth, and the Destiny laboratory in orbit. The rover, Moon base and Mars base interiors are **illustrations generated with NASA photographs as visual references**: SEV, Orion, HDU and CHAPEA for the structures, Apollo 17 and Curiosity for the landscapes.
- The caption under the body says whether the picture is a photograph or a generated illustration, and links to a NASA page. The original sources, the full credits, which photographs fed which illustration and the exact prompts are in the README and in `GENERATION.md`, both in `public/environments/`. The illustrations are not photographs of real bases or validated NASA designs, and the source images are unchanged.
- The anatomy models keep their attributions, shown under the body. Triangles whose orientation contradicts their normals are corrected on loading, to avoid lighting artefacts.
- The two bodies are textured MakeHuman bodies (CC0) with the anatomy registered inside them. If they cannot load, the BodyParts3D reference bodies take their place. Skin is the default depth. Vessels, organs and the skeleton can be explored. The active system stays opaque and the surrounding anatomy remains faint. The selector lists the meshes supplied by the active atlas, including organs occluded in the current view. The female pelvis and major leg bones come from the female HRA. The reference vessels include the named lower-limb veins from Z-Anatomy. [Model credits](../THIRD_PARTY_NOTICES.md).
- The heart beats at the person's simulated resting pulse, or at the camera pulse while the camera measures it. **Breathing** moves the chest at the person's latest breathing rate measured with the camera (Camera, **Breathing**); without a measurement the chest stays still. Only the rate is measured: the depth and phase of the movement are illustrative.
- With **Mission effects** on, the change of the face and the legs from the fluid shift is exaggerated about tenfold, so that it can be seen.

## Still missing

A full photorealistic double of the body would need suitable textured and rigged models, and a further capture step. The current fit improves how a portrait sits on the generic head, but the hair, the back of the head and the personal shape are still missing. The avatar can demonstrate five environment-dependent movements. Its exercise response is illustrative. A dedicated muscle layer and personalised muscle geometry remain outside this model. The heart follows the camera pulse and the chest the breathing rate measured by the camera; the depth and phase of these movements are illustrative. The plan for realistic bodies is in [ROADMAP.md](ROADMAP.md).

The detection and the fit were checked in the browser with the fictional sample. A capture with a physical webcam still has to be checked on the user's own hardware. The automatic tests check the alignment, the outlines, the capture limits and the projection, not how good the result looks on each face.
