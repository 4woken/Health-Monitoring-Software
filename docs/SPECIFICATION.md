# Scope check against the challenge

Challenge: "Create Health Monitoring Software for Astronauts on Space Missions", NASA Space Apps 2026.
Source: https://www.spaceappschallenge.org/2026/challenges/health-monitoring-software/ (read on 2026-11-11). Difficulty: Intermediate.

This is our own summary of what the page asks for, and of where the current version of Pulsar answers it. **Covered** means the app does it, with simulated data. **Partly covered** means part of the request is missing. **Gap** means the app does not do it.

## Required resources

The challenge's resources tab lists five pages: NASA's Human Research Program hazards page, two more Human Research Program pages, and two pages from the Canadian Space Agency. All five were read on 2026-11-13; the notes are in [RESEARCH.md](RESEARCH.md), in the section "Added 2026-11-13". In the current app:

- The five hazards of human spaceflight are cited on the mood signal.
- 12 of the 15 signals link to their NASA Human Research Program risk page.
- The bone loss rate (1 to 1.5% a month) comes from NASA's bone risk page. It feeds the bone and muscle loading signal and the physiology engine.
- The share of station astronauts with some swelling at the back of the eye (about 70%) comes from NASA's SANS page and appears in the optic nerve signal.
- The 16 sunrises a day on the station come from NASA's sleep page and appear in the sleep signal.
- The neutron share measured on the station (Canadian Space Agency) and the advice to keep a journal (NASA's behavioural health page) are in the notes, but not in the current text of the app.

## Gaps to be honest about

- The mission records are simulated. The self-tests measure the person at the computer and feed the signals, but they are screening versions, not clinical tests: a screen is not an eye chart, a webcam is not a force platform, and the limits that flag a result are our assumptions. The spacewalk readings of the AR view are a demonstration.
- The app cannot contact flight surgeons or send reports to Earth.
- It gives no treatment or medication advice, on purpose.
- Immune function and nutrition are covered by three signals (immune cells, vitamin D, body mass). The limits for white cells, lymphocytes and body mass are our assumptions; the vitamin D limit follows a NASA protocol.
- The check-up tracks completed tasks, and a self-test ticks its own task. The habitat exercise demo on the avatar stays separate from the person's results.
- The effect of the Moon and Mars on the body is an assumption: no long stay in partial gravity has been measured.
- The check-up planner follows fixed rules. An AI planner can take its place through the same interface, but none is built.
- People added and their check-ups stay in one browser. Nothing is shared between devices or crew members.

### What this version does

The crew introduction and 3D profile setup lead to one dashboard with five habitats, body dimensions, vital signs, an anatomy explorer and a daily check-up. A short exercise demonstration reflects the current habitat and gravity. Its simulated pulse and breathing are independent of webcam measurements.

### Development order

1. **Done: the main demonstration path.** A recognisable habitat, a complete body, the vital signs, a click on a place, a chart and the steps of each measurement, and a daily progress that rewards the checks done.
2. **Next: day-to-day follow-up.** A level for each part of the grouped signals, reminders, procedures for measuring again, a treatment log filled in from a prescription, and a way to ask for advice about a given signal.
3. **Later: experimental extensions.** Clinical validation of webcam estimates, a fuller avatar, suit pressure, gravity phases and a profile for remote communities.

This order is a proposal for the work ahead; it does not mean that every idea is an official requirement of the challenge. The additions make the data easier to read. They do not validate the measurements or the rules clinically.

Still to build and validate: a log of prescribed treatments, suit pressure, cabin temperature, a history of changing gravity, a retinal exam, a validation of breathing from the webcam, and a profile for remote communities. The webcam face is a portrait projected on the reference head: it does not personalise the internal anatomy. None of these missing items is presented as working.
